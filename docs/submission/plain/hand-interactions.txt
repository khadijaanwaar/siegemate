Siegemate is played with bare hands from the first screen to the result card, through WebXR hand tracking. Touch controllers are an alternative, never a requirement.

Pinch detection: my own tracker measures thumb tip to index tip. A pinch starts under 1.5 cm and ends past 2.5 cm, so it does not flicker at the edge, and it rides out tracking gaps of up to 0.15 s.

Ordering a figure: a pinch within 3 cm of one of your figures takes hold of the order. Its legal squares light up, a green line runs from the figure to your fingers, and a ring marks the square under them. Open your hand over a lit square and the figure marches there. If the hand is lost mid-order, nothing is played.

At a distance: with the hand's ray on the board, a pinch works the same way along the ray, or as a click on the figure and then on the square. A release is judged by where the ray was 100 ms before you opened your hand, because opening the hand swings it. A ray swept across the board only plays the square it was pressed on, or a lit square it rested on for 150 ms. A pinch with the ray off the board does nothing.

Panels: buttons are poked with a fingertip (a 7 mm press, re-armed after backing out, never pressed from behind) or pressed along the ray. Every button has a hover state, a pressed state and a short sound. Targets are at least 22 mm, with 12 mm between them. A pinch that starts on a panel never reaches the board behind it.

Two hands: pinch both edges of the board within 0.3 s of each other and within 10 cm of it to move, turn and resize it (0.6x to 1.6x). Nothing moves until the hands have travelled 2 cm, turned 5 degrees or spread 5%, so closing two pinches never nudges the board.

The system gesture: a pinch with the palm facing you is the Quest menu gesture, and the game ignores it.

Teaching: one hint at a time ("Look at your table", "Pinch to place", "Pinch a soldier, drop on a glow", then the two-hand grab), each waiting until you have done it. With controllers in hand the hints speak of the trigger and grips instead.

Controllers: the trigger and grips feed the same code as the pinches, and a player can switch between hands and controllers mid-game.
