Passthrough mixed reality: a WebXR immersive-ar session in the Meta Quest browser stands the chess board on your real table, and each battleground restyles the real room with a veil that darkens and tints the passthrough (never past 50%), weather falling around you, and the board's light pooling on the table.

Hand tracking: pinch a figure to order it, pinch along the hand's ray from a distance, poke panels with a fingertip, and pinch both edges of the board to move, turn and resize it.

Hit-test: finds a real surface within reach and at table height to place the board on. With no table found, the board stands at seat height.

Touch controllers and haptics: trigger, grips, thumbstick and A/X do everything hands do, with a short pulse on taking an order, playing it and pressing a button.

System recentre: the reference space's reset event keeps the board on its spot of the table when you hold the Meta button.

Session visibility: opening the Quest menu or taking the headset off pauses the clocks, the computer's search and the sound.

Frame rate and foveation: a 72 Hz target frame rate (updateTargetFrameRate) and fixed foveation.

Multiview stereo rendering through super-three, Meta's fork of three.js (not yet checked on a device, since the emulator cannot run it).

Immersive Web Emulator (IWER): every mixed reality test runs in it with scripted hands and Touch controllers.
